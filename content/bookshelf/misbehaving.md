Misbehaving tells the story of how behavioral economics challenged the idea that people always make perfectly rational choices. Richard H. Thaler shows how mental accounting, the endowment effect, and small nudges can shape decisions in everyday life and in markets.

I read this book while studying behavioral finance, and the finance examples stayed with me most. It is a useful reminder that good decisions often depend not only on having the right information, but also on designing choices in a way that helps people act on it.
